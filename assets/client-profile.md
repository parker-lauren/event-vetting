# Client profile — collect this before scoring anything

Ten minutes with the right two people beats an hour of inference. Marketing can answer the ICP section; **finance must answer the commercial section** — that is the point of asking.

## Commercial inputs (from finance)

| Input | Why it matters | Common trap |
|---|---|---|
| Gross margin % | Sets how much revenue a dollar of spend must return | Company-wide vs product-line — ask which |
| Payback target (years) | The window the spend has to pay back in | Often expressed in months; convert |
| **Win rate — actual trailing 12 months** | Drives required pipeline | The number people quote first is usually the aspirational one. Ask for actual, then ask again. |
| Win rate — planning / aspirational | For the comparison view | Keep it, but do not lead with it |
| Average contract value / ARR per customer | Converts pipeline into deal counts | New-business ACV, not blended with expansion |
| Meetings-held → opportunity rate | Converts opportunities into a meeting target | Rarely known. Start at 30% and flag it as an estimate until real data exists. |

If they cannot produce an actual win rate, that is itself the finding: the program cannot be measured, and fixing that is more valuable than any single event decision.

## ICP definition (from marketing and sales)

- Revenue or employee threshold that makes an account qualified
- Geographies in scope, and which have seller coverage
- Industries in and — more usefully — explicitly **out**
- Buying committee titles, split into economic buyer / champion / evaluator
- Named Tier 1 target accounts, if a list exists
- Anti-ICP: who they explicitly do not want. This question surfaces more than the ICP question does.

## Competitive set (for the sponsor-page research)

- Direct competitors, by product name as it appears on a sponsor list
- Adjacent-category vendors who sell to the same buyer
- The client's own partners and resellers — their presence is a positive signal, not a negative one

## Program context

- Total annual event budget, and what it does and does not include
- What is already committed or auto-renewing
- Approval thresholds and who signs at each level
- Executive availability by name, and realistic travel appetite — usually the binding constraint, and almost never budgeted
- Sales capacity for pre-event outreach, in hours per event
- Where leads currently go after an event, step by step

## Definitional decisions to lock down

Make these once, write them down, apply them everywhere:

1. Revenue at **face value** or **FX-converted**, and at what rate
2. Delegates with **blank revenue**: excluded from the denominator, or counted as below the bar
3. ICP prospect = revenue threshold **alone**, or revenue **plus** a decision-relevant title
4. Event-**sourced** or event-**influenced** pipeline

Every one of these changes the numbers. None of them has a universally right answer. All of them must be consistent across events or the comparisons are worthless.
